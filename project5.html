01   Overview

Synchronized 2-D / 3-D UAV Navigation Plan Visualization

This tool displays a UAV navigation plan — Control Station → Clamshell Peak → Mount Wilson Observatory → Control Station — over the Eaton Fire area of interest in the San Gabriel Mountains, CA. It renders the route side-by-side on a 2-D geographic map and a 3-D geographic globe using MATLAB's Mapping Toolbox (geoaxes, geoglobe, geoplot3), then flies a synchronized chase camera along the mission while live trajectory, speed, and attitude analysis plots update in real time. It was built for Group Project #3 (Supporting Infrastructure Design) in UASE 501 — Intro to UAV.

03   Vehicle Performance & Kinematics

Ground speed from commanded airspeed + wind

V⃗_g = V⃗_a(V_a, ψ) + V⃗_wind

Commanded true airspeed is interpolated per leg (slower on climb-out and final approach, cruise speed on the ridgeline transit) and vector-summed with a constant assumed wind to get ground speed and the time vector along the route.

Bank angle (coordinated turn) & flight-path angle

φ = atan2(V_g · dψ/dt, g) γ = atan(climb rate / horizontal speed)

A point-mass approximation derives roll from the coordinated-turn relation and pitch from the climb-rate-to-ground-speed ratio. The waypoint route has sharp corners that a real autopilot would fly as smooth curved turns/climbs; movmean smoothing removes that artifact so the attitude profile reads as an achievable trajectory rather than an instantaneous discontinuity.

04   Display Layout

Three-Panel Synchronized Interface

  • 2-D Flight Path (left): geoaxes with topographic basemap — UAV track, start/end markers, points of interest, campsites, and a live current-location marker with a real-time lat/lon/distance/altitude/heading readout.
  • 3-D Terrain View (center): geoglobe with satellite basemap — the route rendered in 3-D via geoplot3, with a chase camera (campos/camheading/campitch) that follows the vehicle 250 m above track height, pitched down 20°, heading-locked to the current course.
  • Trajectory / Speed / Attitude Analysis (right): a 3-D spatial trajectory colored by AGL altitude, a speed profile (V_a vs. V_g against stall/max-speed limits), and a roll/pitch/heading time history with bank and pitch limit lines — all with live time-cursor markers synced to the flight-through.

Both the 2-D and 3-D current-location markers, the globe camera, and the analysis-panel cursors update together on every playback step, so the geographic view, terrain view, and engineering plots stay in lockstep throughout the flight-through.

05   Requirements & Known Limitations

What This Model Assumes

  • Requires MATLAB's Mapping Toolbox (geoaxes, geoglobe, geoplot3, camera navigation functions) and internet access for basemap/terrain imagery tiles.
  • No DEM was supplied for the AOI, so ground elevation at each waypoint is an assumed value rather than a measured terrain height.
  • Wind is modeled as constant in speed and direction across the entire route rather than varying spatially or with altitude.
  • Geoglobe marker styling is limited to filled circles ('o') — points of interest are distinguished by color rather than marker shape, unlike the 2-D map.
The route order, waypoint set, and vehicle performance limits follow the Group Project 3 mission prompt (UASE 501, Supporting Infrastructure Design, Sec. 2.0 Aerosonde performance data).
